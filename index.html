About

I'm a JD candidate at Yale Law School and an Editor on the Yale Law Journal. My work sits at the intersection of artificial intelligence, national security, and technology policy. I'm a Paul & Daisy Soros Fellow and Harry S. Truman Scholar.

Before law school, I was a Schwarzman Scholar at Tsinghua University in Beijing, where I earned a Master of Global Affairs. I graduated summa cum laude and Phi Beta Kappa from Wake Forest University with degrees in Mathematics and Economics as a Stamps Scholar.

I've worked on AI engineering and policy across government and industry, including roles at OpenAI, Citadel, the Federal Reserve Board, the Office of the Director of National Intelligence, and the U.S. Department of State, where I led a team of 18 engineers developing LLMs for embassy workflows. My current research focuses on AI governance, biosecurity risks from large language models, and the geopolitical implications of emerging technologies.

I write on AI policy, security, and technical standards for publications including Foreign Affairs, MIT Technology Review, The Washington Post, and Lawfare. I also advise organizations including Harvard Kennedy School and previously served as an advisor on emerging technologies at the U.S. Embassy in London.
